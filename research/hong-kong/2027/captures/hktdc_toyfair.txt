EU Expands Exemptions from Portable Battery Replacement Rules
Nov 2026
The EU has published Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1770, expanding the categories of products whose portable batteries may be removed and replaced by independent professionals rather than end-users. The changes concern wearables, certain electric toys and several specialised products. For Hong Kong traders supplying battery-powered goods to Europe, the measure offers targeted flexibility ahead of the battery removability requirements taking effect on 18 February 2027. However, it does not provide a general exemption for sealed products or eliminate the need to make batteries replaceable.
EU Consults on Tighter Chemical Limits for Toys, with Lilial Ban and Lower Bisphenol A Threshold
Oct 2026
The European Commission has opened a public consultation on a draft directive amending Annex II to the Toy Safety Directive 2009/48/EC, running until 15 October 2026, ahead of a planned Commission adoption in the fourth quarter of 2026. For Hong Kong toy manufacturers and exporters selling into the EU, the draft signals a further tightening of the chemical safety rules that already govern allergenic fragrances, bisphenol A, phenol and preservatives in toys, and is a timely reminder to review formulations and testing protocols before the new limits take effect.
No Further Reduction in Statutory Lead Content Limits for Paints and Children’s Products
Jul 2026
The CPSC has determined not to revise the current maximum lead content limits for children’s products as well as paints and similar surface-coating materials.
Import Ban on Foreign Toy Drones Rescinded
Jun 2026
The U.S. Federal Communications Commission announced on 15 June the removal of toy drones and toy drones that contain foreign-produced components from the list of equipment and services that have been determined to pose an unacceptable risk to U.S. national security or the security and safety of U.S. persons. As a result, the ban that had been in place on the importation of these products into the U.S. – including Chinese Mainland and Hong Kong toy drones – is no longer in effect.
