Introduction
The International Conference of Computational Methods and Experiments in Materials Characterisation ran successfully for nine years (Santa Fe, New Mexico, in 2003; Portland, Maine, in 2005; Bologna, Italy, in 2007; New Forest Campus of the Wessex Institute in 2009; Kos, Greece, in 2011; Siena, Italy, in 2013; Valencia, Spain in 2015; Tallinn, Estonia in 2017 and Lisbon, Portugal in 2019). In 2021, it incorporated contact themes and was organised online because of the pandemic. The series continues in 2027 having adopted a shorter title for the event and including mechanical contact which is not only a potential cause of component failure but also a quite common characterisation technique.
All papers to be published in the conference volume as well as those from previous meetings are permanently archived at https://library.witpress.com where they are available to the international community.
Objectives
The aim of the conference is to facilitate interactions within the research community and generate discussion on the latest developments in the rapidly advancing field of materials science and engineering. The meeting is a response to the demand for high quality products for both industry and consumers. An important step towards achieving this is material characterisation, namely, the determination of the physical and chemical properties of materials used.
Consideration of different experimental techniques as well as computer simulation methods is essential to achieve a proper understanding. A very wide range of materials, starting with metals through polymers and semiconductors to composites, necessitates a wide spectrum of experimental characterisation techniques and constitutive theories.
Surface treatment, in particular, enhances material properties; contact mechanics allows the determination of the in-service behaviour of components subject to contact conditions. Component durability is assessed by the understanding of surface engineering, that is, wear, frictional and lubrication dynamics.
Current research on modification technologies can increase the surface durability of materials. The characteristics of a material system reveal which surface engineering methods should be chosen and, as a consequence, it is essential to study contact mechanics in conjunction with the application of surface treatment.

Location
Oslo, the 1,000-year-old capital of Norway, sits on the country’s southern coast at the head of the Oslo fjord. This stunning setting gives hints of the wild wonders that lie just beyond the city. Oslo offers a unique mix of world-class museums, cutting-edge architecture, and outdoor adventures just minutes from the city centre. Walking on the roof of the Oslo Opera House, visiting the Munch Museum, or exploring the National Museum, which houses the iconic original painting of The Scream, are unforgettable cultural experiences. Several major sights sit on the Bygdøy Peninsula, including the waterside Norwegian Maritime Museum, the Holocaust Centre, and a unique ski museum. Other highlights include the Holmenkollbakken, a ski-jumping hill with panoramic views of the fjord, the massive Vigeland Sculpture Park, the new Nobel Peace Centre, and harbourfront floating saunas like SALT. Food and drink can be enjoyed at the vibrant food stalls of Mathallen Oslo in the Vulkan area, right next to the trendy Grünerløkka district. The Oslo Pass allows free travel on public transport, free parking, and entry to many museums and sights.
Conference Venue
The conference will be hosted at Oslo Metropolitan University (OsloMet) in Norway. As one of the country's largest higher education institutions, OsloMet is renowned for its practical, career-oriented approach. The university stands out for several key strengths:
Elite Professional Programmes: A premier choice for applied fields, OsloMet offers top-tier degrees in nursing, social work, teaching, and occupational therapy that directly support Norway's welfare state.
Hands-On Expertise: With a strong emphasis on practical application over pure theory, fields like engineering and computer science prepare students for direct, seamless entry into the workforce.
Dynamic Urban Campus: Situated right in the heart of downtown Oslo, the university provides a vibrant and well-connected city-centre experience.

Oslo Airport Gardermoen is located about 50 km north of the city centre. You have a few quick and convenient options to get to OsloMet:
By Train
Board the Train: Head down to the airport station beneath the terminal. You can take either the Flytoget (Airport Express Train) or a Vy Regional Train heading toward the city centre.
Get off at Nationaltheatret Station: This is the closest major underground railway station to OsloMet.
Duration: Around 22 to 27 minutes.
Walk to OsloMet: Exit the station toward Universitetsgata or Nationaltheatret / Parkveien, head northwest onto Frederiks gate, and walk up toward Pilestredet. Pilestredet 46 is about a 10–12 minute walk uphill.
Ticket Prices:
Flytoget: Adult: Approx. 268 NOK, Student / Youth (under 20) / Senior: Approx. 134 NOK
Vy Regional Train: Adult: Approx. 134 NOK, Child / Senior: roughly half price
By the Airport Bus (Flybussen)
Board the Bus: Take the Flybussen (Route FB5) from right outside the airport terminal.
Get off at Holbergs plass or Dalsbergstien: These stops are closest to Pilestredet 46.
Duration: About 55–60 minutes depending on traffic.
Walk to OsloMet: From Holbergs plass, it is a short 3-to-4-minute walk straight to Pilestredet 46.
Ticket prices vary depending on whether you purchase online in advance or on board, typically ranging between 180 NOK to 250 NOK for a standard adult ticket, with discounts available for children, students, and seniors.
Taxi from Oslo Airport (OSL) to Pilestredet
Taking a taxi from Oslo Airport (Gardermoen) to the Pilestredet area typically costs between 1,000 NOK and 1,400 NOK (roughly $95-$135 USD / €90-€120 EUR), depending on the time of day, traffic conditions, and the specific taxi company you choose. Travel Time: Around 35 to 45 minutes (depending on traffic).
